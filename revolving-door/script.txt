When you picture *foreigners* living in *Okinawa*,
you probably think of *American military*.
But statistically, *one in five* foreign residents on the island
is actually from *Nepal*.
These aren't *military families* or *corporate transfers*.
They're overwhelmingly *young, single students*
packed into urban *language schools*.

It all starts in *Nepal*,
where local *language academies* compete fiercely for students.
Their entire *business model* relies on securing
notoriously difficult *Japanese student visas* for their clients.

For years, *Tokyo's* strict immigration checkpoints
were approving only *10 to 20%* of these applications.
But down in *Okinawa*,
the local immigration offices were approving *nearly 90%*.

Spotting this data, *Nepalese schools*
immediately *partnered* with *Okinawan academies*.
They built a *high-volume*, dedicated *pipeline* to the island
for *guaranteed visa success* and *cheaper tuition*.

But for most of these students,
Okinawa is merely the *entrance door*.
After finishing their *two years* of language school,
they migrate straight to major cities like *Tokyo*
to find *higher-paying* part-time work.

So today, a *bureaucratic quirk* has turned
a *tropical Japanese island*
into the busiest *revolving-door gateway* for *Nepalese youth*.
